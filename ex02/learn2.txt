
Jacobsthal sequence → WHICH element should I insert next?

---

        Numbers
        ↓
        1. Make pairs
        ↓
        2. Compare each pair, separate small and big elements
        ↓
        3. Recursively sort the larger elements from each pair using merge-insertion sort,
            creating a sorted sequence S
        ↓
        4. Insert at the beginning of S the element that was paired with
            the first (smallest) element of S
        ↓
        5. Insert the remaining smaller elements in a specific order
            using binary search

Make Pairs

Compare each pair so the smaller is first, larger is second:
    (3, 5)  (7, 9)   4

The 4 is odd, so it has no partner. Keep it aside for later.

🧩 PmergeMe step by step

Take:
    3 2 1 6 5 9 7 11

1️⃣ Make pairs

    (3,2)
    (1,6)
    (5,9)
    (7,11)

    Put smaller first:

    (2,3)
    (1,6)
    (5,9)
    (7,11)

    Think:

    (small, big)

---

2️⃣ Sort the pairs using BIG values
    Look only at:
        3
        6
        9
        11

    They are already ordered.

    So:
        (2,3)
        (1,6)
        (5,9)
        (7,11)

    If they weren't ordered, you'd reorder the whole pairs.

    This is important because:
        small stays attached to its big partner

---

3️⃣ Create two chains

    From:
        (2,3)
        (1,6)
        (5,9)
        (7,11)

    Big numbers become:

        vectorMainChain:    3 6 9 11

    Small numbers become:

        vectorPending:      2 1 5 7


    PAIR
    ↓
    (2,3)
    ↑ ↑
    │ └── MainChain
    └──── Pending

---

4️⃣ Insert first pending number
    You already know:
        2 < 3

    because they originally came from:
        (2,3)

    So insert 2:

        Before:     3 6 9 11

        After:      2 3 6 9 11

---

5️⃣ Insert the remaining pending numbers

    Remaining:   1 5 7

    You insert them into the correct position.

    For example:

        Main:
            2 3 6 9 11

            insert 5

        Find:

            2 3 [5] 6 9 11

        You don't search manually from beginning to end.
        You use binary search.

---

🧍 What about an odd number?

    Suppose:
        3 2 1 6 5

    Pairs:
        (2,3)
        (1,6)

    leftover:
        5

    Your class stores:
        hasOdd = true;
        oddValue = 5;

    Then after processing the pairs, you insert:

        5

    into the correct position.
